# Turn intent into a bounded behavior contract

Investigate facts available in the project before asking the user. Separate current behavior, desired behavior, assumptions, and unknowns. Agree the current slice, not every hypothetical future feature.

Use concrete normal, rejected, and uncertain-result examples from the accepted request, including relevant screenshot annotations. For a flow crossing screens or asynchronous states, trace the affected entry, action, completion signal, and supported exit/return paths to what the user should then see. Do not equate request acceptance with completion or invent live polling when the accepted flow uses a separate status view. Consider duplicate submission, missing data, partial failure, permissions, and refresh recovery only when relevant; do not turn coverage examples into mandatory new product features.

For a relevant request outside the ordinary path, distinguish a confirmed prohibition, an exception that an authorized person may accept, and an undecided policy. Do not turn missing support into a refusal rule or silently implement a new capability. Capture the decision owner and which business effects must wait while a material exception is unresolved; use one representative example and its observable outcome rather than enumerating hypothetical requests. Prioritize explicit boundary handling where a wrong action is hard to reverse or costly to recover from.

For unresolved interaction choices, start from the user's task, needed context, frequency and recovery needs; a list of controls does not establish usability. Reuse accepted interaction patterns with their applicability. When uncertainty could cause repeated or costly rework, choose a bounded experiment: representative content for information density, an interactive slice for navigation or editing, or a rendered view for visual direction. A modal, drawer or page is a contextual choice, not a universal rule. Keep visual preferences distinct from observable task failures; settled local changes need no new design ceremony.

Translate domain-expert explanations into only the terms, invariants, state transitions and permissions needed for this slice. Have consequential rules grounded in accepted examples with inputs, actions and observable outcomes; distinguish an expert's confirmed rule from an agent's proposal. Use boundary examples to expose ambiguity (for example, ordered 100, received 60, next receipt 50). Do not silently choose a business policy or treat an unanswered question as acceptance. Technical implementation choices need not be sent back to the expert.

Ask a round of independent decisions whose prerequisites are already settled. Give a recommendation, relevant alternatives, cost and reversibility. Clarify priorities such as delivery timing versus operating burden only when they change the current decision; do not speculate about people's motives. Dependent questions belong after their prerequisite. Respect a preference for one question at a time. Keep optional future decisions out of this slice.

Offer evidence-based disagreement when a suggestion conflicts with project facts or accepted goals. Revise your view when new information warrants it. Follow the authorized decision-maker's informed choice within applicable constraints; insistence is not technical evidence, and accepting a tradeoff does not remove its cost. Do not reopen that choice without new facts or changed conditions.

Record the accepted outcome, non-goals, meaningful constraints, examples, affected boundaries, and verification approach in the existing source of truth. A short conversation can suffice for a small change. Where a requirement spans independently verifiable capabilities, propose a small capability/dependency map before planning implementation.

For a changed requirement, compare the effective old rule with the proposed behavior before implementing dependent changes. Explain verified and uncertain effects on contracts, existing data, tests and active work. Identify the decision needed and recommend a path. Reuse explicit approval already given; do not add a second approval merely for process. Once accepted, update the effective rule and affected examples/checks, identifying the superseded decision without leaving readers to merge contradictory historical notes. Existing test names, issue links or contract identifiers can provide lightweight traceability; no separate database is required.

Continue clarifying while an unresolved material decision changes this slice's acceptance; stop when it does not. If the person cannot answer, suggest a concrete example or bounded investigation that could resolve it rather than repeating the question. Do not silently shrink scope or invent a policy. Low-impact reversible implementation details can proceed under existing authorization. If the user asks for design only, end with the design; do not begin implementation.
